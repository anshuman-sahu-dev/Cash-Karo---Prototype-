Primary flow · FR1–FR7

Two taps, zero re-navigation.

The user is already inside a retailer's app with a product decided. They tap Share, tap CashKaro, and continue checkout exactly where they were - no app-switch, no re-search, no losing the cart.

Why this screen A shopper on any retailer app finds a product. Today, converting them means asking them to leave, remember, and re-search inside CashKaro - three drop-off points before the value is delivered.

Secondary flow · FR8–FR12, FR14

One nudge, only when intent was real.

This never fires on a schedule or on app-open. It fires only when a user viewed a specific store inside CashKaro and left without a click-out - and it's capped hard to protect trust.

Guardrail built in Max 1 nudge per store/day, 2 per user/day, auto-mute after 3 non-converting sends. We watch app-opens-without-conversion as a guardrail so this can't quietly turn into noise.

Alternatives we considered and rejected (Section 9)

Reduces friction to open the app, not to convert intent into a tracked order. Its natural metric - app opens - is exactly the kind the brief warns against: it can rise while transactions don't.

India's e-commerce activity is overwhelmingly mobile-app-native - Flipkart and Amazon India each report over 85% of traffic from their own apps. An extension addresses a shrinking, desktop-only slice of the journey.

Solves pre-purchase discovery, not recovery of already-decided purchases - a different problem than this brief defines. Materially higher build cost and per-query latency for an unproven benefit relative to Share-to-Activate.
